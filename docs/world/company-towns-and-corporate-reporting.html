Company Towns and Corporate Reporting

In company-controlled systems, corporate reporting policy routes all station business through company management rather than the Marshal — the structural reason a federal officer can sit in a system for years and know almost nothing.

Overview

The Federal Republic of Worlds licenses corporations to operate whole systems, and in those systems the company is the practical government. Federal authority does not disappear — a Marshal retains full legal power to enforce federal law even against local authorities — but authority and information are different things, and the company controls the second one.

Details

  • Reports route through management. Company policy requires station incidents to be filed with station management rather than passed to the Marshal. The chain runs to the Station Manager’s office and stops there; what continues onward is what management decides to forward. Attempting to brief the Marshal directly is treated as an internal disciplinary matter.
  • This is lawful, which is what makes it durable. No one has to break a law to keep a federal officer ignorant. The policy does it, and it does it to ordinary incidents as reliably as to extraordinary ones, so the pattern never looks like a cover-up from inside.
  • The Marshal’s dependence is structural. A Marshal in a company system spends most of the job on paperwork about company actions, reported by the company. Independent knowledge requires either an informant, a physical incident too large to file quietly, or someone willing to take a career hit.
  • Station hotels clean with robotics. Automated cleaning is the norm across station accommodation. A human cleaning crew is therefore visible as a deliberate choice to anyone who knows the standard — the machines keep no useful long-term memory of their tasks, but neither do they take instructions about what not to notice.

Cross-Domain Links

  • Politics and Citizenship — the licensing power that makes a company the practical government of a system, and the citizen/resident divide that decides who can appeal to the Marshal at all.
  • Federal Credits — the same licensing lever behind accredited institutions and federal banks.

In Play

Established in [Session 04 - The Case of the Missing Courtesan](…/chronicle/Sessions/Session 04/session-04-the-case-of-the-missing-courtesan.html). Under questioning from Julija Borja, Holger Graeme explained that MacMillian policy required all reports to go through the station manager rather than the Marshal Service, and that the last time he tried to circumvent it, Solveig Kaufer threatened his position. The crew overheard the whole exchange through the office wall. Pia Kadyrov separately supplied the cleaning-robotics norm that made the Royale Hotel human crew legible as deliberate.

World Rules

  • Company policy on a corporate-controlled station requires all incident reports to route through station management (the Station Manager's office), not to the Federal Marshal. Going around it is a disciplinary matter, not a legal one.
  • A Marshal has full legal authority but no independent reporting channel inside a company town. What the Marshal learns is what the company chooses to file. This is lawful and is the single largest constraint on federal oversight at the frontier.
  • Station hotels clean with robotics as standard. A human cleaning crew in a station hotel is anomalous on its face and reads as deliberate to anyone who knows the norm.
